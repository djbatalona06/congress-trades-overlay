Capabilities

The public record, beside the chart.

Every field the STOCK Act requires, placed next to the chart you already have open, so you can read filings without another tab.

On-chart disclosure panel

Reads the ticker from the page and docks a compact panel in the corner. Collapsed, it's one line. Expanded, it's the full filing record.

Four platforms, zero setup

TradingView, Robinhood, Webull and Schwab. Switch symbols in a single-page app and the panel follows — no reload, no configuration.

Bond-to-issuer mapping

Viewing a FINRA corporate bond? Capitol Tape resolves the CUSIP-style symbol to the issuer's equity and shows its disclosures.

New-filing notifications

Watch any ticker. Capitol Tape works through your watchlist in the background every hour and sends a browser notification when a new disclosure appears.

Read-only by architecture

Three permissions: storage, alarms, notifications. No brokerage access, no balances, no positions, no order entry. Nothing to audit but the ticker.

Party, chamber and seat context

Each row shows member, party, chamber, state or district, amount range, trade date, filing date and a link to the official filing portal.

Floating signal box: Quiet, Watch or Hot

A small box you can drag anywhere on the chart. It counts what the filings on screen show: several members trading the same stock within 30 days, both parties buying, a large amount range, a brand-new filing, or a filing that came in past the 45-day deadline. Tap a tag to read why it is lit. It is free and costs no extra lookups. It describes filing activity only; it is not a buy or sell signal.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is this financial advice?

No. Capitol Tape displays public STOCK Act disclosures for information only. It does not recommend buying or selling anything, and a member's trade says nothing about how a stock will perform.

Where does the data come from?

From the Bargo Congress Trades API, which publishes the periodic transaction reports that members of the House and Senate must file under the STOCK Act. Coverage is the most recent ~90 days.

How current are the disclosures?

Members have up to 45 days after a trade to file, so every disclosure is delayed by law. Capitol Tape shows both the trade date and the filing date, and refreshes each ticker at most every 6 hours.

Can it see my brokerage account?

No. It reads only the ticker symbol from the page address or title. It cannot read balances, positions or orders, and it cannot place trades.
